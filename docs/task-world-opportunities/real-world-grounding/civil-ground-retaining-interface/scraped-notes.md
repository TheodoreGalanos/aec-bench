# Civil Ground Retaining Interface Scraped Notes

## Eurocode 7 JRC Page

- EN 1997 applies to geotechnical aspects of buildings and civil engineering works.
- The official parts are EN 1997-1 general rules and EN 1997-2 ground investigation and testing.
- The page notes that actions imposed by ground and groundwater are calculated according to EN 1997.

## FHWA GEC 11 MSE/RSS Manual

- FHWA-NHI-10-024 is a public manual for design and construction of MSE walls and reinforced soil slopes.
- The abstract says the manual reflects current practice for design, construction, and monitoring.
- It is based on LRFD procedures and is intended to help engineers select, design, specify, monitor, and contract MSE/RSS construction.
- The table of contents includes drainage sections, including internal drainage, external drainage, and filtration/drainage component requirements.

## WSDOT Geotechnical Design Manual Chapter 15

- Source checked: https://wsdot.wa.gov/publications/manuals/fulltext/M46-03/chapter15.pdf. Local PDF metadata: title "Chapter 15 Abutments, Retaining Walls, and Reinforces Slopes - Geotechnical Design Manual M 46-03"; 442 pages; modified December 2021.
- WSDOT frames abutments, retaining walls, and reinforced slopes as geotechnical design objects used to minimize wetland construction, widen existing facilities, reduce right-of-way needs in urban areas, and modify or replace existing retaining walls and bridge abutments.
- Required project/geotechnical data include risk or variability in subsurface conditions, construction sequence and staging, surcharge from adjacent structures, easements, backslope and toe-slope geometry, wetlands, right-of-way restrictions, excavation limits, materials sources, tolerable settlement, impacts on adjacent structures, maintenance access, and the analyses to be performed.
- The WSDOT information-needs table explicitly links retaining-wall/reinforced-slope design to internal stability, external stability, global and compound stability, rate-of-construction limits, settlement, horizontal deformation, lateral earth pressures, and bearing capacity.
- Field exploration is required for all walls and reinforced slopes. The chapter requires borings, test pits, hand holes, or combinations at each location, with groundwater, topography, soil/rock units, engineering properties, local hazards, and overall-stability concerns represented.
- WSDOT gives a benchmark-shaped boring rule: for walls shorter than 100 feet, explore near mid-alignment or maximum height; for longer walls, use points generally 100 to 200 feet apart, up to 500 feet only in uniform dense conditions, with closer spacing in variable or unstable ground.
- Soil nail walls require extra exploration behind the wall, about 1.0 to 1.5 times wall height, within the nail zone. WSDOT also calls for a test pit near the wall face to evaluate stand-up time, with photographs at excavation and after 24 hours.
- Anchored/deadman walls require extra borings in the anchor/deadman zone outside the no-load zone or at deadman locations, deep enough to cover likely anchor installation and overall stability.
- WSDOT requires overall stability for all retaining walls and reinforced slopes, including surfaces through the wall mass and the soil below/above the wall where relevant.
- WSDOT drainage logic is explicit: provide drainage where water can build up from groundwater, stormwater, flooding, or tidal influence; if drainage cannot be provided, include hydrostatic pressure in design. Soil nail walls use composite drainage between nail columns; lagging-based cantilever/anchored walls need drainage at the lagging/facing interface unless the system is freely draining.
- WSDOT shoring submittal review asks whether geometry, slope angles, adjacent structures/utilities, design life, right-of-way/easement constraints, stratigraphy against contract boring logs, groundwater, loads, surcharges, and limit states are adequately represented.
- WSDOT wall-system evaluation submittals require an overview/product information plus plan details, including component details, typical plan/profile/section views, batter, corners, radii, settlement tolerance, drainage, facing, reinforcement, and construction details where applicable.

## FHWA GEC 7 Soil Nail Walls Reference Manual

- Source checked: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/engineering/geotech/pubs/nhi14007.pdf. Local PDF metadata: title "Soil Nail Manual - Reference Manual"; subject says it covers FHWA guidelines for design and construction of soil nail walls in transportation projects; 425 pages.
- FHWA describes soil nails as passive drilled-and-grouted elements that transfer tensile load to surrounding ground through bond stresses, interact with facing, require corrosion protection, are load-tested, and are subject to construction QC/QA.
- The manual is aimed at roadway projects and permanent soil nail walls, with a framework using ASD stability calculations to quantify nail loads and slip surfaces, then LRFD checks for service, strength, and extreme-event limit states.
- The document organization is useful for task design: it covers applications/feasibility, construction materials, information required for design, resisting mechanisms and limit states, design procedure, drainage, corrosion protection, contracting/specifications, inspection, proof/verification load testing, and monitoring.
- The performance-specification section shows the actual artifact contract: owner-provided preliminary drawings with wall layout, design criteria, subsurface data, right-of-way limits, utility locations, site constraints, material/testing specifications, required submittals, and review requirements; contractor-provided detailed design and drawings.
- Available-information requirements include contract drawings with design loading, wall layout and dimensions, design loads, displacement limits, geotechnical engineering reports, exploratory borings, observation pits, site investigation data, soil corrosion tests, global stability/deformation analyses where provided, and corrosion-protection class responsibility.
- Design submittals include a narrative, applicable code references, soil/rock strength parameters, unit weights, pullout resistance, material strengths, critical cross-sections with strata/piezometric levels/applied loads, load factors, seismic criteria, indexed calculation sheets, structural facing/nail-head calculations, drainage evaluation, and design drawings.
- Design drawings include plan view, baseline/elevation datum, offsets, wall stationing, nail locations, right-of-way/easements, utilities, adjacent structures, drainage structures, exploration locations, longest nail limit, wall top/base elevations, nail spacing, drainage elements, grade profiles in front/behind the wall, cross-sections, construction sequencing notes, and design parameters.
- Proof and verification testing are first-class outputs: the manual includes schedules, load increments, creep readings, acceptance criteria, and required reporting for soil nail load tests.

## FHWA GEC 4 Ground Anchors and Anchored Systems

- Source checked: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/engineering/geotech/pubs/if99015.pdf. Local PDF metadata: FHWA-IF-99-015, June 1999, 304-page public manual.
- The abstract states that the manual covers cement-grouted ground anchors and anchored systems for highway applications, including flexible anchored walls, anchored slopes, landslide stabilization, and tiedown-anchor structures.
- The manual explicitly covers subsurface investigation/lab testing, anchor principles, anchor load testing, construction material/method inspection, design earth pressure selection, anchor design, corrosion protection, wall component design for lateral/vertical loads, overall anchored-system stability, seismic design, two detailed design examples, and technical specifications.
- Anchored soldier-beam and lagging walls are described as common US systems, constructed top-down with soldier beams, lagging, staged excavation, ground anchors, stressing/lock-off, permanent facing, and drainage elements.
- Groundwater and drainage are central: anchored soldier-beam and lagging walls generally avoid large water loads, so surface/subsurface water collection and drainage are part of the design package.
- The design examples provide benchmark-like input/output shapes: wall height, stratigraphy, traffic surcharge, apparent earth-pressure diagram, anchor inclination, soldier-beam spacing, lagging thickness, passive resistance/embedment, axial load, permanent facing design, and slope-stability factor of safety.
