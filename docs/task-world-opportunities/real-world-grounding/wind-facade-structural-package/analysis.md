# Wind Facade Structural Package Analysis

## Real Workflow Chain

The chain is realistic, but it should be treated as a facade load-takeoff and fixing design package, not a pure wind-pressure calculation:

site/location/hazard basis -> terrain/exposure/topography/importance -> design wind action -> facade zones and pressure coefficients -> panel tributary areas -> bracket reactions -> substrate/fixing capacity -> load combinations and utilization -> facade structural note.

ASCE 7 and EN 1991-1-4 establish the wind-action side. EN 1993 and related material standards support steel brackets, connections, and fixings, but proprietary facade systems and anchor approvals often become decisive in practice.

The facade source base is now stronger on component resistance and project-artifact shape than on completed worked examples. Public curtain-wall and rainscreen summaries reinforce the load-transfer and movement chain. Hilti product pages ground bracket, facade-system, anchor, material, substrate, approval, and installation metadata. The Hilti/BBA certificate and HRD-H anchor data sheets add bracket design resistance, rail-to-bracket fixing constraints, wind-action design responsibilities, anchor resistance/load tables, embedment, spacing, and edge-distance evidence. EJOT/CROSSFIX adds an independent rainscreen substructure family with public product pages, product variants, ETA approvals, wall-bracket screw evidence, fixed/sliding point variants, and project-specific design/calculation obligations. Leviat/HALFEN HCW changes the fixture design by adding a manufacturer engineering-intake source-pack route: the forms bridge facade geometry/load assumptions and manufacturer anchoring selection for edge-of-slab, top-of-slab, and punched-window conditions. The top-of-slab questionnaire provides a real bracket drawing/working-load datum, while the edge-of-slab and punched-window forms expose the required field shapes. SFS/NVELOPE now adds public product-download and source-pack evidence beyond the app route: the 2024 capability brochure documents Project Builder inputs/outputs, drawing overlay, fixed/sliding point behavior, level 1/2/3 fixing responsibilities, and windload-derived bracket centre calculations. The extracted SFS brochure text also gives concrete bracket option table fields for source packs: standard aluminium vertical bracket sizes and zone-depth ranges with product codes, NVF2F bracket ranges, NH3 slot/product-code rows, and a limited NH3 load-vs-bracket-size chart. Public SFS installation guides and rendered NV1/NV3 detail drawings further sharpen the setout/detail packet shape: they bind bracket positioning and fixing locations to static calculations, define fixed/sliding point rules, expose primary/secondary fixing product fields, specify pull-out-test and expansion-gap boundaries, and provide generic section/detail-location drawings for cladding zones, window interfaces, corners, isolators, support brackets, insulation, ventilated cavity, cladding/rivet callouts, and a generic NV3 panel-module elevation with hanger/horizontal-rail callouts. The SFS product-page and BBA pass materially improves the capacity side: NV, NH3, and NVF2F public BBA certificates expose design-resistance tables, rail/profile section properties, BS EN 1991-1-4 wind-action basis, site pull-out-test responsibility, and a combined vertical/horizontal utilization rule. SFS public case studies add real-project context around that source-pack structure: Project Builder design-service use, accurate wind-load calculation and pricing statements, detailed design statements, real cladding areas, panel mass, wall deviation, bracket ranges, additional bracket counts, custom rail lengths, selected NVELOPE system families, pull-out/performance-test context, and downloadable case-study PDFs. Cascadia Clip adds the strongest current public bridge between calculator workflow and structural source fields: the spacing calculator points users to structural-capacity checks, the RDH design briefs define load-table variables and the controlling fastener-force check, the IAPMO report gives allowable per-spacer shear/compression data and installation/substrate constraints, the fastener/girt sheets provide rail and fastener source-pack fields, and the rendered assembly layout guide provides a public visual clip-spacing and installation-sequence artifact. Direct inspection of the Cascadia calculator implementation adds query-level evidence: exact option codes, endpoint query order, result tuple mapping, chart-data arrays, and unsupported/unavailable sentinel states. A current Cascadia product-download and quote-form pass adds 3-part specification, installation, architectural detail, adjustability, BIM, project quote, product selection, and generic AutoCAD detail drawing evidence, but the public route still stops before the digital spreadsheet, formal calculator export, project submittal, real elevation, selected clip layout, or governing fastener/spacer utilization. Fixfast and Downer strengthen the UK app/intake side: Fixfast exposes calculation inputs and a calculation-request webform with drawing upload and fallback field logic, and the SPIDI Max/MechFix product pages and brochures now add bracket length/material/code fields, fixed/sliding/drillscrew/anchor location diagrams, substrate-specific component schedules, material-grade standards, static/dynamic calculation wording, detailed calculation and schematic/assembly drawing outputs, and materials/pricing schedule evidence. Downer Designer and the Downer download-centre form describe project-specific static/thermal calculations, fixing analysis, optimized component/fixing configurations, NBS specification, integrated wind-load analysis, PDF outputs, downloadable manual/BBA-certificate routes, maximum rail-length static-calculation output, cantilever optimization, and peak pull-out/shear fixing advice. BICEP and Clad-Sec strengthen the technical-guide and site-checklist side: BICEP contributes an Australia-facing bracket adjustment/fixed-floating-point source and project engineering request fields, while Clad-Sec records the expected structural calculations and setout drawings needed before installation. The remaining weak link is a redistributable worked facade package that ties a real elevation and wind-pressure schedule to selected bracket rows, substrate/anchor checks, and utilization.

The Eurofox/Rainscreen Works pass improves the public sample-calculation layer. The Eurofox catalogue shows a sample static-analysis sheet with input data, a wind-load basis, profile length, cantilever, effective span, unit spacing, facade element, support system, wall weight, sliding-point verification, anchor-point verification, bolt/rivet shear checks, dowel-force checks, moment and deflection diagrams, stress ratio, and explicit utilization percentages. Hilti Ireland and Williaam Cox/Rainscreen Works independently describe the service output as static calculations, elevation drawings, installation plans, setting-out information, components schedules, material lists, and fastening-point coverage. That makes the source-pack/check shape much clearer, while still leaving the real-project elevation and issued calculation package unresolved.

The WPS structural-calculation pass adds a public system-calculation chain for US/California variants. The WPS package is not only a product page: it lays out exterior wall cladding panel assemblies, connection spacing, Zone A/C tributary areas, panel dead loads, ASCE 7-10/CBC wind pressures, seismic out-of-plane forces, backing conditions, adjustable mounting bracket components, connection summary capacities, material properties, and screw allowable load tables. It is especially useful for a task-owned calculation packet that checks the trace from code basis to tributary demand to fastener capacity. It still does not supply a real facade elevation, project pressure-zone map, or issued project-specific utilization package.

The Knight Wall Systems pass adds a second US/North American route that is valuable because it exposes how a manufacturer frames project engineering and submittals, not because it closes the issued-calculation gap. KWS MFI evidence records spacing governed by design wind pressure, cladding weight, deflection requirements, and substrate type; guide specifications require manufacturer Professional Engineer project-specific calculations for bracket spacing; guide notes tie maximum spacing and dead load to wall-anchor live/dead load, tension, pry, and shear; and KWS states that project-specific engineering can specify anchor type, embedment depth, and spacing in project engineering packages. The D-Series THERMABRACKET data sheet adds bracket/cavity/depth/adjustability/material fields, while the ANKIT anchor data sheet adds substrate-specific installation, pull-out/tension, spacing, and edge-distance values. KWS case studies add real project/product-selection context, but they still stop before a public elevation, pressure-zone map, selected support/anchor schedule, and final utilization chain.

The Hohmann & Barnard pass adds a complementary North American manufacturer route because it explicitly combines engineered support service wording with public load-table fields. HB RS EZclad is described as fully engineered to project conditions by H&B engineers and as including structural connection, rail, engineering calculations, and shop drawings. HB RS FLEXbracket is a stainless steel universal bracket ordered to suit insulation depth and vertical or horizontal rail orientation. The public HB Rainscreen Brochure gives load-table structure for a task-owned bracket-source pack: 30 psf wind load, dead load up to 5 psf, bracket spacing combinations, wind-load demand in pounds, exterior structural steel stud screw pull-out values, Fu/Fy steel strength fields, gauge fields, and checkmark pass rows. This improves bracket load-table and shop-drawing/calculation-output shape evidence, but it is still a source-pack/service boundary rather than a returned project calculation.

The Downer project-context pass clarifies a UK route without changing the benchmark-readiness state. Targeted searches for returned facade static-calculation outputs, filled Project Builder reports, planning attachments, and facade-tied bracket/anchor utilization examples did not recover a redistributable calculation packet. The public Downer project archive and representative pages do add real project names, locations, and selected-system context for DCS031, including AC Hotel by Marriott at Manchester Salford Quays with Futural A1 and Downer DCS031, and Broxbourne School with DCS031 bracket and rails. This supports a `downer_project_context_static_calc_request_packet` that pairs project/product context with the existing Downer static-calculation request form, but the task still needs a task-owned or public returned calculation, drawing overlay, pressure-zone schedule, selected fixing schedule, and utilization chain before it is benchmark-ready.

The near-term benchmark construction path should therefore be a task-owned redrawn source pack. The pack should not invent a fake vendor report; it should materialize the public artifact shapes already captured into a redistributable project scenario. A minimal hard fixture would include: an original/redrawn elevation with panel grid, edge/corner zones, openings, bracket rows, and substrate callouts; a pressure schedule by zone and height; selected bracket/support rows from a normalized SFS/H&B/KWS/Cascadia/Fixfast/Downer-style source table; selected anchor/substrate capacity excerpts from a Hilti/ANKIT/WPS-style table; an anchor geometry and edge-distance sheet; a calculation table deriving tributary areas, dead load, wind suction/pressure, bracket reactions, anchor demand, and combined utilization; and a short memo explaining governing component, pass/fail result, and source excerpts used. This makes the benchmark source pack redistributable and inspectable while preserving the evidence boundary that no public returned project package has been found.

## Facade Redrawn Source-Pack Contract

The file-level contract for `facade_redrawn_calculation_source_pack` should be:

- `project.json`: region or authority basis, units, load-combination labels, wind-pressure assumptions, drawing references, fixture version, and source-family provenance for each embedded excerpt.
- `elevation-redrawn.svg` or `elevation-redrawn.pdf`: task-owned elevation with panel grid, dimensions, body/edge/corner zones, openings, bracket rows, rail lines, substrate callouts, datum lines, and detail tags.
- `geometry-oracle.csv`: drawing bounds, pressure-zone extents, opening extents, support-point coordinates, scale mapping, and drawing-to-schedule cross-reference roles.
- `pressure-schedule.csv`: zone, height band, pressure or suction, load combination, tributary rule, and governing pressure flag.
- `support-schedule.csv`: support family, bracket or clip product row, rail/profile row, fixed/sliding role, centre spacing, tributary width/height, fastener count, and demand fields.
- `capacity-excerpts.yaml` or `capacity-excerpts.csv`: bracket resistance, rail/fixing resistance, anchor/substrate values, embedment, edge distance, spacing, backing material, and public source-family tag.
- `anchor-check-oracle.csv`: selected anchor row, substrate, embedment, edge distance, nearest-anchor spacing, tension/shear demand, resistance, utilization, geometric pass flags, and governing anchor check.
- `calculation-oracle.csv`: panel or bracket location, tributary dimensions, dead load, wind load, bracket reaction, anchor demand, bracket utilization, anchor utilization, combined utilization, pass/fail, and governing flag.
- `expected-output.md`: required annotated elevation and calculation memo shape, including governing component explanation and explicit references to embedded source-pack excerpts.
- `verifier-implementation-brief.md`: docs-only future verifier contract covering source files, staged checks, variant handling, diagnostics, acceptance evidence, and non-runtime boundary.
- `variant-matrix.yaml`: docs-only future fixture variants for richer openings, re-entrant corners, anchor geometry failures, anchor demand mismatches, and over-limit utilization cases.
- `variant-source-overlays.yaml`: row-level overlay values for the variants named in `variant-matrix.yaml`, including added geometry/support/calculation/anchor rows and negative-case table mutations.
- `variants/variant-source-files.yaml` and `variants/*/`: docs-only expanded SVG/CSV source files for selected pass/fail variants, derived from the overlay file and still outside runtime benchmark execution.
- `verification-rules.yaml`: tolerances, allowed unit conversions, required drawing/table cross-references, formula checks, failure localization, and pass/fail criteria.

The verifier should run staged checks for manifest completeness, drawing geometry extraction, pressure-zone assignment, tributary load derivation, support-table lookup, anchor edge-distance/embedment validity, combined bracket/anchor utilization, governing-row identification, and memo traceability. It should fail responses that rely on live vendor data, ignore embedded excerpts, skip drawing-to-table consistency, or state a pass without identifying the governing bracket or anchor component.

## Seed Skeleton

A docs-only seed skeleton now exists at `facade_redrawn_calculation_source_pack/`. It is intentionally small: one task-owned 12 m by 9 m redrawn elevation, a geometry oracle, three representative support rows, three pressure zones, normalized source-family capacity excerpts, an anchor-check oracle, a calculation oracle, expected output instructions, verifier rules, a verification-case matrix, a variant matrix, variant source overlays, and expanded docs-only variant source files. The seed uses `BR-B01`, `BR-E01`, and `BR-C01` to exercise body, edge, and corner zones without pretending to be an issued project report.

The oracle is deliberately transparent. Each representative support uses a 0.60 m by 1.50 m tributary area, so the table can check area, wind load, dead load, bracket utilization, anchor tension/shear utilization, combined anchor utilization, and governing component. A local self-check confirmed that the manifest files exist, the JSON parses, every support label appears in the redrawn SVG, schedule joins resolve, and the oracle values match the formulas within a 0.005 tolerance. This moves the facade path from file-contract prose to a concrete research skeleton, but it still leaves executable verifier implementation, richer drawings, multiple failure cases, and benchmark integration unbuilt.

The follow-on verifier-case pass adds `verification-cases.yaml` and updates the manifest, expected output, and verifier rules. The case matrix now has one baseline pass case and localized negative cases for missing capacity files, missing drawing labels, invalid pressure zones, tributary-area mismatch, missing anchor lookup, underreported governing utilization, over-limit rows incorrectly marked pass, live vendor lookup, issued-report misrepresentation, and missing governing memo. A second local self-check confirmed the verification-case YAML parses, every negative case names an expected failure stage covered by `verification-rules.yaml`, and the original oracle still checks out. This is still research-only: it gives future verifier implementation concrete targets but does not implement the verifier.

The geometry-oracle pass adds `geometry-oracle.csv` plus scale metadata in `project.json`. The oracle records the SVG drawing bounds, five pressure-zone strips, one opening extent, and support-point coordinates for `BR-B01`, `BR-E01`, and `BR-C01`; it also gives future verifier cases explicit drawing-bounds, opening-extent, and support-coordinate failure targets. A local self-check now confirms the geometry file is listed in the manifest, required by the verifier rules, consistent with the SVG viewBox, and aligned with support schedule zones. This narrows the richer-geometry gap, but it still does not implement OCR/vision extraction, multi-opening geometry, setbacks, re-entrant corners, or runtime verifier code.

The anchor-check pass adds `anchor-check-oracle.csv` for the selected `ANCH-CONC-M8` row. It records substrate, embedment, edge distance, nearest-anchor spacing, tension and shear demand, resistance, utilization, pass flags, and the governing anchor check for each representative support. The verifier cases now include anchor edge-distance, embedment, and demand-trace failures, giving future implementation a concrete distinction between missing anchor lookup, invalid anchor geometry, and wrong demand transfer. This improves the selected-anchor schedule and anchor-check layer, but it is still a docs-only oracle rather than an issued anchor design report or executable verifier.

The variant-matrix pass adds `variant-matrix.yaml` so future fixture generation has named target cases instead of an open-ended "make it richer" instruction. The matrix preserves the current baseline and defines future added-opening, re-entrant-corner, anchor-edge-distance, anchor-spacing, and over-limit-corner-pressure variants, with expected pass/fail result and localized failure stage where applicable. The follow-on overlay pass adds `variant-source-overlays.yaml` with exact row-level additions and updates for those variants: a second opening and adjacent edge support, a re-entrant-corner pressure/support row, anchor edge-distance and spacing failures, and an over-limit corner-pressure failure. The expanded-source pass adds `variants/variant-source-files.yaml` and concrete docs-only SVG/CSV files for those non-baseline variants, including two visual geometry variants and three negative table variants. The verifier-brief pass adds `verifier-implementation-brief.md`, which maps the source files to future executable stages, variant selection rules, diagnostic fields, and acceptance evidence. This is stronger fixture-generation scaffolding, but it still does not implement executable verifier logic or runtime benchmark integration.

The current NVELOPE Project Builder and Hilti PROFIS pass improves the live app/report boundary without closing the drawing-level evidence gap. NVELOPE Australia exposes a current two-step cladding calculator and designers/installers checklist route with project fields, local wind-load and cladding-zone dependencies, elevation and plan AutoCAD drawing expectations, and report contents including static calculations, setting-out information, thermal calculations, and indicative pricing. The current SFS UK Project Builder page confirms a beta customer input form and login/register boundary rather than an anonymous filled output. Hilti Australia documents PROFIS Engineering as anchor/baseplate design software with AS 5216:2021 and AS 4100 context and report access, while a public PROFIS report demonstrates report mechanics: anchor inputs, load-combination results, anchor reaction tables, tension checks, shear checks, combined utilization, and pass/fail-style status fields. This should be used to shape Project Builder intake/report-boundary fixtures and anchor-report fixtures, not as proof of a facade-specific Project Builder output or rainscreen utilization package.

The Ancon/Leviat masonry-support pass adds a useful adjacent facade-support/restraint branch. It is not a rainscreen wind-pressure package, but the public MDC/Optima pages, design sheet, Optima layout drawings, installation guides, and masonry-support/lintels literature show how a manufacturer turns facade support conditions into cavity/load/fixing/layout decisions. The evidence exposes BS EN 1993 intake fields, standard Optima unfactored-load classes, load/cavity tables, fixing-type branches, edge/slab assumptions, torque and shim rules, brickwork bearing, MDC syntax, stone support syntax, and individual cleat syntax. This is strong enough for task-owned masonry-support load/cavity/layout fixtures, while still leaving the drawing-level wind-zone, bracket/anchor utilization, and Project Builder/report gap open.

## Harness Implications

- The verifier should check consistency across drawings, pressure tables, bracket layouts, and utilization, not only final pressure.
- Strong failure modes include wrong zone mapping, sign convention errors, loss of internal pressure, using panel area instead of tributary bracket area, and ignoring eccentricity.
- A robust task instance needs at least one visual artifact: elevation or facade grid. Without that, the task collapses into a scalar wind formula chain.
- Manufacturer product metadata and public approval/data-sheet evidence are now useful for source-pack construction and variant generation. The verifier should still grade against task-provided capacity excerpts or normalized tables so the evaluation does not depend on live vendor PDF retrieval.
- Manufacturer questionnaires support a source-pack schema with a branch for known dead/wind loads and a fallback branch that derives those loads from geometry, system weight, and wind pressure/suction inputs. The top-of-slab HCW working-load values can be used as a task-provided capacity excerpt, not as a live vendor dependency.
- The SFS evidence supports three fixture types: a `project_builder_request_packet` with project inputs and expected output fields, a `project_builder_output_packet` with bracket/rail spacing, selected bracket table rows, static calculation excerpts, drawing overlay, and fixing quantities, and a `facade_system_detail_drawing_packet` with generic cladding detail-location drawings, section details, component callouts, adjustment tolerances, fixed/sliding point checks, primary/secondary fixing references, generic NV3 elevation/hanger/datum-line evidence, and expansion/pull-out-test boundaries. Public sources currently ground request/output shape, bracket-table lookup fields, and detail/setout drawing structure better than the actual filled project-specific static calculation output.
- The SFS evidence supports three fixture types: a `project_builder_request_packet` with project inputs and expected output fields, a `project_builder_output_packet` with bracket/rail spacing, selected bracket table rows, static calculation excerpts, drawing overlay, and fixing quantities, and a `facade_system_detail_drawing_packet` with generic cladding detail-location drawings, section details, component callouts, adjustment tolerances, fixed/sliding point checks, primary/secondary fixing references, generic NV3 elevation/hanger/datum-line evidence, and expansion/pull-out-test boundaries. - Ancon/Leviat supports a `masonry_support_load_cavity_packet` or `facade_masonry_support_layout_packet`: task-owned excerpts can carry BS EN 1993 design-sheet inputs, Optima load/cavity/fixing tables, standard layout drawing assumptions, MDC/stone/cleat specification syntax, shim/torque/bearing rules, and explicit flags for missing real-project drawing and utilization evidence. This should stay adjacent to, not merged with, the rainscreen Project Builder output gap.
- Current NVELOPE and Hilti sources support two additional packet shapes: `nvelope_project_builder_current_intake_packet` for live AU/UK Project Builder fields, drawing expectations, report contents, and login/no-filled-output boundaries, and `profis_anchor_utilization_report_packet` for anchor report tables, governing load combinations, reaction tables, utilization statuses, and explicit non-facade provenance. These packets should be combined only when a task-owned source pack supplies the missing facade elevation, pressure zones, selected bracket schedule, and anchor schedule.
- The natural verifier split is four-stage: wind-pressure schedule, facade drawing/tributary extraction, bracket/subframe resistance, and anchor/substrate capacity. A failure in one stage should be explainable without masking errors in the others.
- A strong fixture can be created synthetically if it embeds a small bracket-capacity table and anchor-capacity table derived from public-style source fields, while using a generated or redrawn facade elevation to avoid project confidentiality.
- Vendor workflow applications such as SFS/NVELOPE Project Builder are better treated as meta-harness/source-pack inspiration than as runtime dependencies. They imply realistic inputs such as project requirements, wall substrate, cladding system, bracket family, and engineering support outputs, but the benchmark should materialize those fields inside the source pack.

## Multimodal Extension

- Inputs: facade elevation image/PDF, wind-zone diagram, bracket detail, fixing datasheet, small BIM/IFC extract.
- Outputs: annotated elevation, extracted load table, bracket reaction table, anchor/substrate check, flagged governing bracket/anchor, and calculation memo.
- Interesting harness checks: image-to-table consistency, zone boundary detection, bracket count and spacing verification, fixed/sliding point interpretation, capacity-table lookup, anchor edge-distance/embedment validation, and evidence linking from visual mark-up to numeric table.

## Meta-Harness Opportunities

- Reconfigure regional basis: ASCE 7, Eurocode, AS/NZS 1170.2.
- Swap facade system: cassette, curtain wall, rainscreen, stone cladding, PV facade.
- Mutate geometry: add corners, parapets, setbacks, re-entrant corners, or mixed substrate.
- Combine with structural connection or embodied-carbon tasks by passing bracket loads downstream.
- Reconfigure source-pack completeness: product page only, product page plus certificate, full normalized table, or deliberately conflicting drawing/table pack.
- Reconfigure product workflow mode: static approval excerpts, manufacturer product pages, app-shaped project-builder briefs, or a combination where a meta-harness pass converts vendor evidence into normalized task tables.
- Combine with fire/cladding-safety tasks by adding cavity barrier and combustibility evidence, while keeping the wind/fixing verifier distinct from fire compliance.
